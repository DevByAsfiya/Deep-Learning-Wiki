# Books

| Book | Covers | Level | When to read it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *Deep Learning* (Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville) | Foundations and theory | intermediate | As a reference, not cover to cover. Part I is a solid maths refresher |
| *Dive into Deep Learning* (Zhang et al.) | Everything, with runnable code beside every concept | beginner | The best free option. Available in PyTorch, TensorFlow, and JAX |
| *Hands-On Machine Learning* (Géron) | Classical ML then deep learning | beginner | Read the classical ML half before starting deep learning |
| *The Elements of Statistical Learning* | Statistical foundations | advanced | When you want to understand why a method works, not how to call it |
| *Designing Machine Learning Systems* (Huyen) | Production ML systems | intermediate | When you first need to ship rather than train |

## A note on reading

Most deep learning books go stale in the applied chapters within two years and
stay useful in the foundational ones for decades. Weight your reading
accordingly: buy books for the fundamentals, and read documentation and papers
for anything current.
